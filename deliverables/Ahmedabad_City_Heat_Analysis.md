# Macroscopic Urban Heat Analysis: Ahmedabad City Level

## Executive Summary
This report provides a macroscopic analysis of the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect across the entire Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation boundaries. Using a high-resolution 100m x 100m physics-informed grid, we have generated comprehensive city-wide spatial distributions of **Land Surface Temperature (LST)** and the **Socio-Economic Vulnerability Index (SEVI)**.

By analyzing the city holistically rather than by individual wards, we can identify critical large-scale structural heat corridors and optimize the deployment of the 7-Lever Cooling Architecture (such as Vernacular White Roofs and Amrit Sarovar water bodies).

## 1. Land Surface Temperature (LST) Hotspots

![Ahmedabad LST Map](file:///c:/Users/tanya/OneDrive/Desktop/ps1ohm/deliverables/Ahmedabad_LST_Map.png)

> [!WARNING]
> **Key Finding:** The highest LST anomalies are heavily clustered in the **Eastern and South-Eastern Industrial Zones** (e.g., Vatva, Odhav, Naroda).

### Interpretation
* **The "Concrete Heat Sink" Effect:** The eastern periphery shows extreme thermal retention. This is driven by high built-up density (`ghsl_height`), massive industrial tin-roof complexes, and a severe lack of vegetative cover. 
* **The "Blue-Green" Cooling Corridor:** Conversely, the western zones and the central Sabarmati River corridor demonstrate significantly cooler thermal profiles, confirming our localized hypothesis that water bodies (Class 0 LULC) provide a localized Park Cool Island (PCI) effect.

### Recommended Interventions
* **Targeted Albedo Modification:** The massive flat industrial roofs in the east are the perfect candidates for **High-Albedo Slaked Lime / Ceramic Mosaic** applications. Deploying this vernacular cooling strategy here will yield the highest absolute temperature drop per rupee spent.

## 2. Socio-Economic Heat Stress (SEVI) Vulnerability

![Ahmedabad SEVI Map](file:///c:/Users/tanya/OneDrive/Desktop/ps1ohm/deliverables/Ahmedabad_Heat_Stress_Map.png)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Finding:** Heat *exposure* does not perfectly map to heat *stress*. The SEVI map reveals that while the industrial edges are physically hotter, the **Central and South-Central Residential Wards** (e.g., Danilimda, Gomtipur) face the highest acute vulnerability.

### Interpretation
* **Density Multiplies Heat:** SEVI models physical heat combined with population density and adaptive capacity. The extreme vulnerability (top 1% cyan clusters) in the central zones is driven by dense, low-income tenement housing with high anthropogenic heat emission (`qf`) and low access to cooling resources.
* **Lack of Micro-Refugia:** These areas lack localized "cool spots" for respite during peak daytime heat.

### Recommended Interventions
* **Community Evaporative Hubs:** These ultra-dense vulnerability hotspots require immediate micro-climate interventions where roof-space is limited. Deploying **IoT Misting Stations (Chilled Water @ 7.7°C)** and reviving historic **Amrit Sarovar Ponds / Stepwells** with passive terracotta shading will create critical pedestrian cool zones (-3°C to -4°C) for the highly exposed local population.

## Conclusion
The city-level analysis validates the necessity of a *hybrid* cooling policy. While the Eastern industrial belt requires vast structural albedo modifications (White Roofs), the central dense residential wards require acute, localized evaporative cooling (Misting and Water Bodies) to mitigate human vulnerability. The 7-Lever Optimization Engine successfully balances these competing requirements based on this exact spatial data.
